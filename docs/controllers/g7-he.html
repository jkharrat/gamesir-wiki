Which app, and the exact platform wording

GameSir Nexus (Xbox and PC; a 'Legacy GameSir Nexus' build also exists). The G7 HE does not use GameSir Connect, which is the app for the G7 Pro line. One gamepadla commenter reports Nexus failing to surface the newest firmware, matching GameSir's own instruction to fall back to a standalone PC upgrader.

How these measurements were arrived at

Gamepadla has no full stick test for the G7 HE, only a linearity test, so center error, circle error, asymmetry and measured bit resolution are unavailable. That linearity test (firmware 6.33, cable, XInput) measured 91.5% linearity, 4.3% tremor, 172 total positions and 0.00580 step resolution. GameSir's own marketing claim of '0 Fluctuation, 0 Deviation and 0 Circularity error' is unverified marketing language, not a measurement.

Trigger detail

No physical trigger stops or trigger locks; Windows Central calls this out as a specific omission. Hair-trigger mode is toggled by holding M + LT or M + RT for 2 seconds until the corresponding trigger vibrates. GameSir claims '100% response area'. Trigger travel distance is not documented.

Mapping the extra buttons

Two rear paddles (L4/R4), a front 'M' (Mode) button below the d-pad, and a mic mute button. Unlike the G7 SE, the G7 HE has NO physical lock latches for the back buttons: GameSir's product spec list omits the 'Back Button Quick Latches' line that the SE spec list carries, Windows Central specifically flags the removal, and PCGamingWiki and comparison tables agree. Be aware GameSir's own G7 HE manual page still says 'two lockable back buttons' and even refers to 'the G7 SE' in its body text, which appears to be copy-pasted from the SE manual. Remap by holding M + R4/L4 for 3 seconds until the indicator blinks slowly, then tapping the target button.

Which modes work on which platform

Wired only, via a 3 m detachable USB-C cable. No battery and no wireless mode. GameSir's product page additionally lists Steam as a supported platform; the manual tech-spec block also lists Android, but this is not repeated on the product page or FAQ and is unconfirmed.

Polling rate caveats

GameSir states the Xbox console enforces a 250 Hz cap so 1000 Hz is PC-only, and support advises locking to 250 Hz if you use the 3.5 mm headset jack. Gamepadla verified roughly 988 Hz, 499 Hz and 250 Hz operating points on firmware 6.40. GameSir's published Windows 11 / firmware 6.30+ / Nexus 1.2.9+ prerequisites for 1000 Hz are written for the G7 and G7 SE; whether identical prerequisites apply to the HE is not separately documented.

Gyro detail

GameSir's official gyroscope list does not include the G7 HE, and gamepadla's verified specs also record no gyroscope.

Battery detail

Wired only. GameSir's spec sheet lists 'Battery: No' and gamepadla's verified spec list records 'No battery'. There is no battery-life claim to attribute, measured or manufacturer.

Faceplate detail

Magnetic swappable faceplate with painting support. G7 series faceplates fit the newer G7 Pro, but the G7 Pro's three-part split faceplate does NOT fit the G7, G7 SE or G7 HE. Calibration must be performed with the faceplate installed or the stick range values will be wrong.

Known issues and fixes

Documented problems with documented fixes.

Controller is connected and lit but registers no input, disconnects constantly, or will not power on.
Likely causePID recognition failure between controller and host, or the controller sitting in the wrong input mode (XInput vs GIP).
FixWhile connected, hold the Xbox/Home button + M button for 3 seconds (an indicator tone should sound), then unplug and replug to restore the original PID and device recognition. If that fails, hold the Xbox/Home button + Share button for 3 seconds while connected to force-switch to XInput. Also move to a rear motherboard USB port rather than a case port or hub, and swap in a verified data-capable cable.

Source: gamesir.com

Random disconnects, failure to power on, or dead inputs traced to the installed firmware.
Likely causeGameSir names firmware 6.33, 6.38 and 6.5.6 as known problematic versions for the G7 SE and G7 HE that cause connection-related issues.
FixCheck your version in GameSir Nexus (the 'Firmware Upgrade' arrow at top right) or Legacy GameSir Nexus ('Updates' on the controller page) and update to a current stable build. If Nexus does not offer the update, use GameSir's standalone manual upgrader for G7 HE/SE on a PC.

Source: gamesir.com

Microswitch face buttons or the d-pad double-click or send the wrong input; a bumper auto-clicks on its own.
Likely causeCorrupted mapping/profile state or outdated firmware.
FixHold the Xbox button + Menu button for 5 seconds while connected to run the function reset, then update firmware to the latest version in GameSir Nexus (GameSir's page cites V6.40 as the target at time of writing), falling back to the standalone PC upgrader if Nexus does not show it.

Source: gamesir.com

3.5 mm headset produces no audio and the mic does not work.
Likely causeReport rates above 250 Hz disable the controller's onboard audio by design; secondary causes are an incompletely inserted headset plug or an outdated firmware build.
FixGameSir support says the polling rate in GameSir Nexus on PC 'should be locked at 250hz to avoid potential problems'. Push the headset plug fully flush, confirm the mic is not muted (red LED near the bottom of the pad, or double-tap M to toggle), and on Xbox set Settings > General > Volume & audio output > Headset audio to 'Stereo uncompressed'. If it persists after a firmware update, test a different CTIA-standard headset.

Source: gamesir.com

Sticks appear to drift straight out of the box after the initial deadzone is set to 0 in GameSir Nexus.
Likely causeNot hardware drift. Zero deadzone is a true physical zero, making the stick hypersensitive to residual movement.
FixGameSir's official answer is to set the initial deadzone to roughly 3-5 rather than 0. For genuine stick or trigger faults, run the manual Stick & Trigger Calibration and the function reset (Xbox + Menu held 5 seconds).

Source: gamesir.com

Controller becomes unresponsive or refuses to power on after a button combo was held while the cable was inserted.
Likely causeHolding Xbox + Menu, or View + Xbox + Menu, WHILE plugging in enters DFU (firmware update) mode or manual calibration mode instead of normal operation. GameSir also warns that cable guards can make a half-seated cable feel fully inserted.
FixUnplug, reinsert the cable firmly with no buttons held until it clicks, then hold the Xbox button to power on. If still unresponsive, perform a manual firmware upgrade from a Windows PC using GameSir's standalone G7 series upgrader.

Source: gamesir.com

Back buttons are pressed accidentally during normal play.
Likely causeThe G7 HE removed the two physical quick-latch lock switches that the G7 SE has on its back buttons, so the paddles cannot be mechanically disabled.
FixNo hardware workaround exists. Clear the mapping instead: hold M + R4 (or M + L4) for 3 seconds until the indicator blinks slowly, then tap R4 (or L4) itself to cancel its assigned value, or use a profile with the back buttons unmapped.

Source: www.windowscentral.com

Frequently asked questions

What is the correct stick and trigger calibration procedure for the G7 HE? (Do NOT use the G7 Pro procedure.)

Hold View + Menu + Xbox and, still holding, plug the USB-C cable into your PC; release once it is in. The indicator blinks slowly white. Without touching LT, RT or either stick, press A and the indicator goes out. Now press LT and RT to full travel and rotate both sticks at maximum angle three times, then press A again — a solid white indicator confirms it. Unplug and replug before using. Calibrate with the faceplate installed or the range values come out wrong, move slowly and steadily, and note that this only affects analog trigger mode. Manual calibration often works where the in-app one fails. This plug-in procedure belongs to the older G7 series (G7, G7 SE, G7 HE) only: GameSir warns that using it on a G7 Pro drops that controller into firmware update mode, after which it will not power on normally. The G7 Pro uses View + Menu + Home held 2 seconds while already connected.

Source: gamesir.com

What exactly is different between the G7 HE and the G7 SE?

Almost everything is shared: both are wired-only, in the same 152 x 103 x 63 mm shell, with the same Hall Effect sticks and analog triggers, four rumble motors, two back buttons, 3.5 mm jack and mute button, magnetic faceplate and Nexus software. Five things differ. The HE has microswitch ABXY and a microswitch d-pad rated for 3 million presses where the SE has membrane; it adds rubberized silicone grips; it drops the SE's two physical back-button lock latches; it weighs 227 g against 221 g; and it launched at $49.99 against $44.99. GameSir publishes its own comparison as an image with no text, so this one is assembled from the two spec sheets plus PCGamingWiki and Windows Central.

Source: gamesir.com

Is the G7 HE wireless, and does it have a battery?

No. It is wired only, using a 3 m detachable USB-C cable, and GameSir's spec sheet lists 'Battery: No'. There is nothing to charge and no battery-life figure of any kind. Windows Central's main criticism of the controller is precisely that it remains wired at a $49.99 price in 2024.

Source: gamesir.com

How do I reset the G7 HE to default settings?

Function reset: hold the Xbox button + Menu button for 5 seconds while connected. This is also GameSir's documented fix for a key bind you set incorrectly in Nexus and cannot undo, and for buttons registering double or wrong inputs. Full reset: hold View + Menu + M until the logo button blinks, then hold the Xbox button for 3 seconds, which resets the profile and all controller settings. You can also simply reapply the default profile with M + Y.

Source: gamesir.com

How do I enable hair-trigger mode, switch profiles, and remap the back buttons?

Hair trigger: hold M + LT or M + RT for 2 seconds until the corresponding trigger vibrates. Profiles: M + Y for default, M + B for Profile 1, M + A for Profile 2, M + X for Profile 3. Back buttons: hold M + R4 or M + L4 for 3 seconds until the indicator blinks slowly, then tap the button you want assigned; the indicator turns solid to confirm. To cancel, repeat the combo and tap R4/L4 itself.

Source: gamesir.com

How do I control headset volume and the game/chat mix from the controller?

A headset must be plugged into the 3.5 mm jack. Hold M + d-pad Up/Down to raise or lower master volume; the G7 HE connection tutorial documents 11 levels while the manual says 10, with a beep at maximum and minimum. Hold M + d-pad Left/Right to shift the balance between game volume and voice chat volume, with a beep at maximum, middle and minimum. Press the mic mute button to mute (indicator turns solid red) and press again to unmute; holding it while muted temporarily unmutes.

Source: gamesir.com

Can the G7 HE reach 1000 Hz polling on an Xbox console?

No. GameSir states the Xbox console enforces a 250 Hz polling rate limit, so the 1000 Hz option is only available on PC. GameSir additionally warns that any polling rate above 250 Hz disables the onboard 3.5 mm audio, and support recommends locking to 250 Hz if you use a headset through the controller.

Source: gamesir.com

Does the G7 HE have a gyroscope or trigger stops?

Neither. GameSir's official gyro-equipped Xbox controller list covers the G7 Pro, G7 Pro Wuchang, G7 Pro Zenless Zone Zero, Tarantula Pro Xbox and T7 Pro Sugar Whirl, and the G7 HE is not among them; gamepadla's verified specs also record no gyroscope and no trigger lock. Windows Central lists the absence of trigger stops as a notable omission for FPS players.

Source: gamesir.com

What can I configure in GameSir Nexus for the G7 HE?

Three controller profiles covering button mapping, stick and trigger tuning and vibration. Sticks get curves, initial and max deadzone, anti-deadzone, a true zero deadzone option, and a switch between Default and GameSir's 'Raw' mode. Triggers get impulse triggers for all games, a hair trigger in Static mode (any movement in range reads as a full 100% pull) or Adaptive mode (100% on entering the range, dropping to 0 on the slightest release), and a Continuous Trigger toggle that holds an input until pressed again. Firmware updates also run through Nexus, with a standalone PC upgrader as a fallback.

Source: gamesir.com
