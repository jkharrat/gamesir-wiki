GameSir Tarantula Pro

A tri-mode multiplatform controller with a symmetric PlayStation-style stick layout, Mag-Res TMR sticks, and a motorized gear that physically rotates the face buttons between Xbox and Nintendo layouts.

High-endGameSir Mag-Res TMR (Gen-1) — tunneling magnetoresistance, non-contact1000 HzGyroGameSir Connect (PC) and the GameSir app (mobile). GameSir Nexus does not support this model. Worth knowing: GameSir's downloads page does not list the multiplatform Tarantula Pro under any application — it lists only the Tarantula 8K PC under Connect and Tarantula Pro for Xbox under Nexus — while the official Tarantula Pro FAQ directs users to Connect. Treat the FAQ as authoritative and the downloads listing as incomplete.

Triggers, D-pad and buttons

Trigger technologyHall Effect analog triggers with a physical gear that converts them to micro-switch triggers
Trigger stopsYes
D-padFour-way tactile-switch D-pad, positioned upper-left in a symmetric DualShock-style arrangement. Not swappable or replaceable.
Face buttonsFour membrane ABXY buttons with no printed symbols. A motorized internal gear physically rotates the cluster between Xbox and Nintendo layouts, visible through a transparent window; the buttons are LED-lit and change color with the selected layout. Reviewers report the combo is M + A (Xbox) or M + B (Switch) held ~2 seconds; GameSir's FAQ states 'hold for 2 seconds until the gear rotates' but shows the combo only as an image. Membrane switches are the most commonly criticized part of the controller.
Rear buttons2
Extra bumpersNot documented

Three states are documented by GameSir: Hall Effect analog mode (linear, for racing), Hall Effect hair-trigger mode (enabled via a key combo — GameSir does not state which combo on the pages reviewed), and micro-switch mode selected by moving the physical trigger gear.

Nine remappable buttons in total: R4, L4 (back buttons), C1, C2, C3, C4, T1, T2, T3. Each can be mapped to a single button or a sequence of up to 12. GameSir's documentation for the Tarantula Pro does not classify T1/T2 — on the Tarantula 8K PC the same T1/T2 names are described as 'extra bumpers,' but a GamesRadar hands-on describes the Tarantula Pro's extra front controls as four small buttons around the center panel plus an actuator on either side of it, so extraBumpers is left null rather than guessed. Physical back-button latches on the rear mechanically disable L4/R4 to prevent accidental input.

Polling rate (Xbox)Not documented

RumbleHD Rumble with 2 motors, per GameSir's official FAQ. Note the 'Tarantula Pro for Xbox' variant instead has 4 motors (one in each grip and each trigger), and the Tarantula 8K PC has none.

Tri-mode: USB-C wired, 2.4GHz dongle, Bluetooth. Variant differences: the 'Tarantula Pro for Xbox' is WIRED-ONLY (3m detachable USB-C, no battery, 4 rumble motors, micro-switch ABXY, 3.5mm jack, 235g) and uses GameSir Nexus rather than Connect; the 'Tarantula 8K PC' is also wired-only (1.8m cable, PC only, 8000Hz, Gen-2 TMR, no rumble, no battery, 180g). The 'With Dock' / Bundle Edition is the same controller shipped with a charging station.

Not an Xbox-compatible model — the separate 'Tarantula Pro for Xbox' is rated 1000Hz on PC and 250Hz on Xbox console. Bluetooth is much slower: Gamepadla measured a 162 Hz median polling rate in Sony/DS4 mode. Gamepadla LatScore is B wired / D wireless, with measured wired button latency of 7.83 ms and dongle button latency of 31.7 ms on firmware 2.0.6 — notably worse than the Cyclone 2.

Gyro: Built-in 6-axis gyroscope, supports motion control on Switch. The controller also has NFC for amiibo interaction with Nintendo devices and can wake the Switch with a single press.

Notable features

  • Motorized gear physically rotates the unlabeled ABXY cluster between Xbox and Nintendo layouts, with a transparent window showing the mechanism
  • Symmetric PlayStation-style stick layout, unusual for a Switch/PC controller
  • Nine remappable buttons, each supporting sequences of up to 12 inputs
  • Five extra buttons on the front face arranged in three rows (C1-C4 and T3) — GameSir's stated 'tarantula' design inspiration
  • Physical back-button latches mechanically disable L4/R4
  • Triggers switch between Hall Effect analog, Hall Effect hair-trigger, and micro-switch via a physical gear
  • NFC support for amiibo plus one-press Switch wake
  • Turbo with four modes: Slow 8Hz, Medium 12Hz, Fast 20Hz, Off
  • On-controller RGB editing by holding M plus a stick or the D-pad (effect, animation speed, brightness, color, saturation)

Known issues and fixes

Documented problems with documented solutions. Each entry cites where the fix comes from.

Double-clicking D-pad, reversed/reverted joystick axes, low polling rate, and the controller not being recognized by GameSir Connect over the dongle.
Likely causeGameSir states there were many bugs in early firmware versions of the Tarantula Pro, in both the controller and the dongle.
FixUpdate BOTH the controller and dongle firmware to the latest version. Controller: connect via USB, open the updater, press Logo + X + Y simultaneously until the device status shows online, then click update firmware. Dongle: exit GameSir Connect, insert the receiver, hold the pairing button for 5 seconds until Device Status shows ONLINE in green, then click Upgrade Firmware. WARNING: GameSir provides different controller firmware files by serial number (V2.06 for SN starting with 1 or 2, a separate V2.06 for SN starting with 3) and warns that the wrong file may brick the controller.

Source: gamesir.com

GameSir Nexus does not detect the Tarantula Pro at all.
Likely causeWrong application. The multiplatform Tarantula Pro is a GameSir Connect device; Nexus is only for GameSir's Xbox-licensed controllers (including the separate 'Tarantula Pro for Xbox').
FixUse GameSir Connect, connected via dongle or cable in XInput mode. GameSir explicitly notes Connect will not detect the controller when it is in DS or NS mode over a Bluetooth connection.

Source: gamesir.com

Apparent stick drift straight out of the box after setting the initial deadzone to 0 in GameSir Connect.
Likely causeNot a defect. A 0 deadzone is a true physical zero, which makes the stick extremely sensitive to any resting-position noise.
FixSet the inner deadzone to 3-5 in GameSir Connect.

Source: gamesir.com

Cannot connect over Bluetooth or dongle; the indicator light keeps blinking slowly.
Likely causeSlow blinking means the controller is in reconnecting mode, trying to reach a previously paired device, rather than in pairing mode.
FixHold the Pairing Button on the controller for 2 seconds to force pairing mode — the light should then blink fast. For a dongle connection, also hold the pairing button on the dongle until it blinks fast so the two re-pair.

Source: gamesir.com

Stick drift or trigger inaccuracy during use.
Likely causeSticks and/or triggers have lost calibration.
FixRun the on-board Stick & Trigger Calibration procedure documented in the manual; GameSir links a video calibration tutorial from the FAQ page.

Source: gamesir.com

Higher-than-expected input latency, especially over the 2.4GHz dongle.
Likely causeFirmware/internal processing rather than polling rate. The controller reports a genuine 1000 Hz polling rate, but measured hardware latency is much higher than similarly specced GameSir models.
FixNo documented fix. Gamepadla measured 7.83 ms average button latency wired and 31.73 ms via dongle on firmware 2.0.6, versus 2.33 ms wired for the Cyclone 2, and rates it B wired / D wireless. Prefer the wired connection for latency-sensitive play and keep firmware current.

Source: gamepadla.com

Frequently asked questions

Which software do I need — GameSir Nexus or GameSir Connect?

GameSir Connect, connected via dongle or cable in XInput mode. GameSir Nexus does not recognize the multiplatform Tarantula Pro. Connect also will not see the controller if it is in DS or NS mode over Bluetooth. The mobile GameSir app can also configure it. (The separate 'Tarantula Pro for Xbox' is the opposite case — it uses GameSir Nexus.)

Source: gamesir.com

What platforms does the Tarantula Pro support?

PC, Switch, iOS, Android, and Steam. It does not support Xbox consoles — that requires the separate wired 'Tarantula Pro for Xbox' model.

Source: gamesir.com

How do I change the face button layout between Xbox and Nintendo?

GameSir's FAQ says to hold the combo for 2 seconds until the gear rotates and changes both the layout and the key values; the default is the Xbox layout. Reviewers document the combo as M + A for the Xbox layout and M + B for the Switch layout. The buttons physically rotate and change color to match.

Source: gamesir.com

How many remappable buttons are there and what can they do?

Nine: R4, L4, C1, C2, C3, C4, T1, T2, T3. Each can be mapped to a single button or a sequence of up to 12. Assignable values include A, B, X, Y, LB, RB, LT, RT, LS, RS, View, Menu, D-pad, and both sticks.

Source: gamesir.com

What do the back button latches do?

They mechanically enable or disable the L4/R4 back buttons so you can prevent unintended input when you are not using them.

Source: gamesir.com

How do I pair the Tarantula Pro to a phone over Bluetooth?

Hold the controller's combo for 2 seconds until channel indicators 1 and 5 flash, then release. Open your phone's Bluetooth list, find 'GameSir- Tarantula Pro' and connect. The channel indicator goes solid when connected.

Source: gamesir.com

Does it have a turbo/rapid-fire function?

Yes, with four modes: Slow (8Hz), Medium (12Hz), Fast (20Hz), and Off.

Source: gamesir.com

How many rumble motors does it have?

Two HD rumble motors. This differs by variant: the wired 'Tarantula Pro for Xbox' has four (one in each grip and each trigger), and the Tarantula 8K PC has none.

Source: gamesir.com
